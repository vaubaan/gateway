The mission

You write what it may do. Not a token that can do everything.

A token pasted into an agent can do anything it allows, for as long as it lives. A mission names each permission from the service’s catalog, gives it a limit, and marks the few actions that wait for your yes.

1

Named, never “all”. Pushing to a feature branch and merging into main are two different permissions.

2

Counted. A looping agent hits its own limit and stops, with a link where you give more.

3

Asked. What cannot be undone waits for you, with the exact arguments in front of you.

On your computer

From download to your agent’s first call, without writing code.

  1. 1

    Install Vaubaan

    A Mac or Windows app. It opens in your browser on your own computer.

  2. 2

    Connect GitHub

    Paste a token you make at GitHub; a guide shows where. It is encrypted here and never given to the agent.

  3. 3

    Tick what it may do

    Read, Propose, Ship, each at a trust level. Vaubaan writes every permission and limit; adjust any of them.

  4. 4

    Give your agent its key

    One button adds the mission to Claude, Cursor or any MCP client. Stop it or replace the key whenever you want.

Rather look first? Open the demo: seeded accounts and missions, simulated services, nothing to connect.

Then your team

Same missions, on a server your team signs in to.

Run the same software with Docker on your network, behind your identity provider. Accounts are connected once and shared permission by permission; nobody hands a token to anybody.

Give access, not tokens

The owner of the GitHub account gives Bob the permissions he may use in his missions. Never more than the owner holds.

One key per person

Teammates on a mission make their own key. Every call carries a name; a person who leaves loses theirs at once.

For the person who has to say yes

What it protects, and what it does not.

Written by the project for your security review, before you ask.

It protects

  • The agent never holds a service credential, not even for git.
  • Credentials encrypted at rest, one key per account, from a master key you keep.
  • Every call decided against the mission and recorded, allowed or refused.
  • An agent cannot widen its mission or approve its own action.

It does not

  • Protect you from whoever runs the server: they hold the master key.
  • Sandbox plugins: they run in the process, signed for who built them.
  • Intercept CLIs or SDKs: only MCP and git through Vaubaan.
  • Come audited: no third-party review yet. It is pre-release.

Questions

Which agents work with it?
Any MCP client that sends a bearer header over HTTP: Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor and others. The mission’s key is that header.
Does my agent ever see my token?
No. Vaubaan runs each allowed call with the credential and returns the result. Git goes through Vaubaan’s own remote, so cloning and pushing need no token either.
Is there a hosted version?
No. It runs on your computer or your server, nowhere else. There is no account to create.
Is it ready for production?
Not yet. Versions are 2.0.0-alpha, breaking changes are expected until 2.0.0, and no third-party audit has happened. The known limitations are listed in the docs.
What does it cost?
Nothing. Apache-2.0, on your own machines.
