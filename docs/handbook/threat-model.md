# Threat model — who can do what to an instance, and what answers it

The security reading of the spec ([vaubaan-gateway](../spec/vaubaan-gateway.md)
§2.4, §3, §6, §10; [gateway-risk-model](../spec/gateway-risk-model.md)); it
adds no rule. Every control names its proof: a test file, or an invariant of
the structural suite (`apps/gateway/tests/unit/structural/manifest.ts`, cited as
`inv:` and its id between brackets). `tests/…` paths are under `apps/gateway/`, `src/…` under
`apps/gateway/src/server/`. Reporting a vulnerability: [`SECURITY.md`](../../SECURITY.md).
The website's trust center parses the "Accepted risks" table and the "Out of
scope" list (`apps/website/src/content/trust.ts`).

Last reviewed against the pre-publication security audit of 2026-10-31. What
it changed: the second factor's step capped and locked (rows "The second
factor guessed…", A43); password hashing off the event loop and a locked
account answered without a hash (row "Anonymous password posts…", A44);
"forgot password" timing (row); the sign-in provider test a guarded form (row);
compression of static assets only (row); the CalDAV home host pinned (row);
plugin calls refuse redirects (hostile service); the Compose hardening, the
image built without root and the entrypoint's links (build rows); a release
run fails a registry outage (A17 narrowed); every checkout without a token
(row); mission-token symbols drawn without bias (assets); the broker's pin
before any write (row, **[inv: broker-key-pinned-before-write]**).

## The trust proposition

One instance, one company, one trust domain: the company's hardware, TLS
terminator and identity provider. Why credentials are stored decryptable by
the instance: ADR [trust-model-clear-at-rest-idp-style](../decisions/trust-model-clear-at-rest-idp-style.md).

- **The operator is trusted.** They hold the master key and can read a decrypted credential in a running call's memory; no control below defends against them. Their acts are **visible** (all audited) and their role **narrow**: configure, approve OAuth clients, keep local groups, deactivate users, reassign ownerless accounts — never read arguments, decide others' requests, or issue missions on accounts they do not hold.
- **The identity provider is trusted for identity** (a signed ID token); authorization is local, from ownership, shares and current memberships.
- **A plugin is trusted code**, and who built it decides whether it runs: the catalog (`vaubaan/gateway-plugins`, rebuilt from reviewed source and signed by its release workflow — what "signed by Vaubaan" means), a company key an administrator named, or an operator's pin ([ADR](../decisions/plugin-signature-sets-the-trust-level.md), [ADR](../decisions/plugins-installed-when-first-needed.md)): a classification error is a vulnerability. The service behind it is **not** trusted, nor is the published versions feed, nor the network an archive travels on.
- **Agents are not trusted**: tool name, arguments, headers and label are attacker-controlled.

## Actors

- **Operator** (host, configuration, master key, admin rule) — must not act without a trace, or be designated by anything but the admin rule. Matching the admin rule adds Administration, nothing on the domain.
- **Account owner** — must not keep ownership after leaving the owner group, or touch an account they do not own.
- **Beneficiary** (a share) — must not exceed it, share further, or keep authority after revocation or narrowing.
- **Mission creator** — must not issue beyond authority, keep a mission serving once authority is gone, or decide another creator's request.
- **Executor** (agent, a mission token) — must not see a credential, exceed the mission, approve or claim for itself, change its mission, or use the other two surfaces.
- **Delegated client** (MCP client) — reads the user's authority and issues missions in their name; must not execute a tool, answer a request, open a gate, touch accounts or shares.
- **Outside attacker**, **hostile service** — trusted for nothing; must not read, authenticate, exhaust, make a signed-in user act, or change a decision.

## Assets

- **Service credentials** (`accounts.credential_blob`, `instance_config` secrets): XChaCha20-Poly1305, one key per row derived from the master key, row id as associated data.
- **The master key**: in the configuration, never the store; a database copy without it opens nothing. Backups hold everything in protected form **as long as the key is kept apart from them**: the reference deployment mounts it on a volume of its own (`/keys`), and the boot warns when the key file shares the store's mount unless `encryption.accept_key_beside_store` says the copies are kept apart (`src/compose/key-placement.test.ts`, `deploy/docker/smoke.sh` step 14).
- **Mission tokens**: shown once; SHA-256 of a 32-character random secret (base62 drawn without modulo bias, `packages/shared/src/api-key.property.test.ts`), constant-time compare, looked up by a non-secret prefix (argon2id rows of earlier builds revoked by migration 0028). Delegated tokens and codes: SHA-256. Web sessions: row key = SHA-256 of the cookie.
- **Sealed arguments of an open approval** (`requests.args_blob`): sealed like credentials, erased on decision and expiry.
- **Authority** (ownership, shares, memberships, policies): explicit rows only (**[inv: enumerations-persisted]**), recomputed at every use.
- **The audit trail**: no update or delete by the application; a risk set goes only with its last entry, inside the purge (**[inv: audit-immutable]**).
- **Budgets and counters**: decided and decremented in one transaction (**[inv: counters-agree-with-audit]**).

## The two credentials, and why they never cross

- **Mission token** → the execution surface (`/mcp`, the git remote); **human session** (`__Host-vbn_session`) → the interface. The door recognises a session **by kind** and answers `wrong-credential-kind`; the request surface refuses any `Authorization` header before reading the session. The management door and its delegated tokens were retired on 2026-11-14 (ADR [platform-management-is-a-plugin-on-the-execution-door](../decisions/platform-management-is-a-plugin-on-the-execution-door.md)); `/mcp/manage` answers 404 to anything.
- Why: **an agent cannot enlarge itself.** An agent that prepares missions holds the key of a mission on its person's Vaubaan account; the `vaubaan` plugin still cannot answer a request, open a gate, extend a budget, reset a period, or touch an account or share — its ports are seven mission methods and static catalogs, nothing else (**[inv: vaubaan-plugin-hard-deny]**). It issues only within the creator's authority and that mission's accepted risks and budget, marked as prepared by that mission; it never rotates nor revokes the calling mission, never issues a mission holding a Vaubaan account, and rotates only missions the calling mission issued.
- Proof: **[inv: two-credentials-never-cross]** (`apps/gateway/tests/integration/execution/credential-separation.test.ts`), **[inv: vaubaan-plugin-hard-deny]**.

### A compromised or hostile service

- **It cannot change a decision**: classification, risk, budget and gate are computed before it is contacted.
- **It can return hostile content to the agent** (prompt injection), forwarded as is: the injected agent still holds only its risks, budget and gates, cannot approve its own gated action, and is audited — why gates exist on irreversible risks.
- **It cannot redirect a credential elsewhere**: every plugin call states `redirect: 'error'` (or a reasoned `manual`/`follow`), checked by the Semgrep rule `vaubaan-plugin-fetch-without-redirect-policy`; a redirect to another origin would otherwise carry custom credential headers (Supabase `apikey`).
- **It can lie in discovery**: an invented ref grants nothing until an owner's mission selects it. **It can reject or stall**: a rejected credential turns the account `expired`.
- **A stolen service credential** is outside the gateway's view; the service's logs are the trail (A30).

## Out of scope

- The security of the host, the TLS terminator, the IdP and the service
  accounts themselves.
- An agent's own behaviour within its mission (what it does with what it is
  allowed to read).
- The legacy v4 application: gone from the tree, with its documents (tag
  `v4-final`).

## Procedures — keeping this document true

- A new control gets a row **with its proof**; a control without a test is an accepted risk until it has one.
- A new accepted risk gets the next number, a reason and its limit; removing one means the control exists: move it to its surface or mark it **Retired**, keeping the numbering.
- `apps/gateway/tests/unit/structural/index.test.ts` fails when a cited `[inv: …]` is unknown, a named test file is missing, or the A-numbers are not consecutive; the website build fails when "Accepted risks" or "Out of scope" disappears.
