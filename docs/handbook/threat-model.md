# Threat model — who can do what to an instance, and what answers it

The security reading of the spec ([vaubaan-gateway](../spec/vaubaan-gateway.md)
§2.4, §3, §6, §10; [gateway-risk-model](../spec/gateway-risk-model.md)); it
adds no rule. Every control names its proof: a test file, or an invariant of
the structural suite (`apps/gateway/tests/unit/structural/manifest.ts`, cited as
`inv:` and its id between brackets). `tests/…` paths are under `apps/gateway/`, `src/…` under
`apps/gateway/src/server/`. Reporting a vulnerability: [`SECURITY.md`](../../SECURITY.md).
The website's trust center parses the "Accepted risks" table and the "Out of
scope" list (`apps/website/src/content/trust.ts`).

## The trust proposition

One instance, one company, one trust domain: the company's hardware, TLS
terminator and identity provider. Why credentials are stored decryptable by
the instance: ADR [trust-model-clear-at-rest-idp-style](../decisions/trust-model-clear-at-rest-idp-style.md).

- **The operator is trusted.** They hold the master key and can read a decrypted credential in a running call's memory; no control below defends against them. Their acts are **visible** (all audited) and their role **narrow**: configure, approve OAuth clients, keep local groups, deactivate users, reassign ownerless connections — never read arguments, decide others' requests, or issue missions on connections they do not hold.
- **The identity provider is trusted for identity** (a signed ID token); authorization is local, from ownership, shares and current memberships.
- **A provider plugin is trusted code**, shipped and reviewed: a classification error is a vulnerability. The provider behind it is **not** trusted.
- **Agents are not trusted**: tool name, arguments, headers and label are attacker-controlled.

## Actors

- **Operator** (host, configuration, master key, admin rule) — must not act without a trace, or be designated by anything but the admin rule. Matching the admin rule adds Administration, nothing on the domain.
- **Connection owner** — must not keep ownership after leaving the owner group, or touch a connection they do not own.
- **Beneficiary** (a share) — must not exceed it, share further, or keep authority after revocation or narrowing.
- **Mission creator** — must not issue beyond authority, keep a mission serving once authority is gone, or decide another creator's request.
- **Executor** (agent, a mission token) — must not see a credential, exceed the mission, approve or claim for itself, change its mission, or use the other two surfaces.
- **Delegated client** (MCP client) — reads the user's authority and issues missions in their name; must not execute a tool, answer a request, open a gate, touch connections or shares.
- **Outside attacker**, **hostile provider** — trusted for nothing; must not read, authenticate, exhaust, make a signed-in user act, or change a decision.

## Assets

- **Provider credentials** (`connections.credential_blob`, `instance_config` secrets): XChaCha20-Poly1305, one key per row derived from the master key, row id as associated data.
- **The master key**: in the configuration, never the store; a database copy without it opens nothing. Backups hold everything in protected form **as long as the key is kept apart from them**: the reference deployment mounts it on a volume of its own (`/keys`), and the boot warns when the key file shares the store's mount unless `encryption.accept_key_beside_store` says the copies are kept apart (`src/compose/key-placement.test.ts`, `deploy/docker/smoke.sh` step 14).
- **Mission tokens**: shown once; SHA-256 of a 32-character random secret, constant-time compare, looked up by a non-secret prefix (earlier rows argon2id until first use). Delegated tokens and codes: SHA-256. Web sessions: row key = SHA-256 of the cookie.
- **Sealed arguments of an open approval** (`requests.args_blob`): sealed like credentials, erased on decision and expiry.
- **Authority** (ownership, shares, memberships, policies): explicit rows only (**[inv: enumerations-persisted]**), recomputed at every use.
- **The audit trail**: no update or delete by the application; a risk set goes only with its last entry, inside the purge (**[inv: audit-immutable]**).
- **Budgets and counters**: decided and decremented in one transaction (**[inv: counters-agree-with-audit]**).

## The three credentials, and why none crosses

- **Mission token** → the execution door `/mcp`; **delegated token** → the management door `/mcp/manage`; **human session** (`__Host-vbn_session`) → the interface. Each door recognises the other two **by kind** and answers `wrong-credential-kind`; the request surface refuses any `Authorization` header before reading the session.
- Why: **an agent cannot enlarge itself.** One process may hold a mission token _and_ its creator's delegated token; the management door still cannot answer a request, open a gate, extend a budget, reset a period, or touch a connection or share — it is handed seven mission methods, static catalogs, the admission function and its rate limiter, nothing else (**[inv: management-door-hard-deny]**). It issues only within the user's authority, marked as delegated, and rotates only missions its own delegation issued.
- Proof: **[inv: three-credentials-never-cross]** (`apps/gateway/tests/integration/oauth-as/credential-separation.test.ts`), **[inv: management-door-hard-deny]**.

### A compromised or hostile provider

- **It cannot change a decision**: classification, risk, budget and gate are computed before it is contacted.
- **It can return hostile content to the agent** (prompt injection), forwarded as is: the injected agent still holds only its risks, budget and gates, cannot approve its own gated action, and is audited — why gates exist on irreversible risks.
- **It can lie in discovery**: an invented ref grants nothing until an owner's mission selects it. **It can reject or stall**: a rejected credential turns the connection `expired`.
- **A stolen provider credential** is outside the gateway's view; the provider's logs are the trail (A30).

## Out of scope

- The security of the host, the TLS terminator, the IdP and the provider
  accounts themselves.
- An agent's own behaviour within its mission (what it does with what it is
  allowed to read).
- The legacy v4 application: gone from the tree, with its documents (tag
  `v4-final`).

## Procedures — keeping this document true

- A new control gets a row **with its proof**; a control without a test is an accepted risk until it has one.
- A new accepted risk gets the next number, a reason and its limit; removing one means the control exists: move it to its surface or mark it **Retired**, keeping the numbering.
- `apps/gateway/tests/unit/structural/index.test.ts` fails when a cited `[inv: …]` is unknown, a named test file is missing, or the A-numbers are not consecutive; the website build fails when "Accepted risks" or "Out of scope" disappears.
