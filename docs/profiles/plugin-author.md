# A plugin author who models a service for others to tick

A developer who connects a service Vaubaan does not cover yet: its tools and, above all, its
catalog of permissions, the lines other people will tick. Public through the catalog, or private
for their company's internal service. The job: add the service in an afternoon with their agent,
without reading the gateway's code. The research counts them among those who pay without
receiving: they model a whole service and live neither the silence nor the yes. Desk research
only; a first outside author shipping a plugin would validate it.

## Pains

- **Having to read the gateway's code** — modelling a service should not require knowing the server.
- **A guide that assumes the cloned repository** — two competing guides, the longer one stale.
- **No way to try or submit in one command** — trying a call without a gateway, running a local gateway, signing and submitting are still planned.
- **Trust that never comes** — a plugin earns trust from use, but few instances report and nothing shows it ran for real.

## Gains

- **My plugin runs on a real instance** — activated, on trial, by an administrator.
- **A service plugged in an afternoon** — my agent does most of it, guided by the kit's skills.
- **Private for my company, or public in the catalog** — the same plugin, my choice.
- **Proof that it works** — the plugin's page shows it ran against the real service.
