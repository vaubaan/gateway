Connecting a provider means giving the instance, once, a credential for an account at a service — a key created by hand, or the service's own sign-in — so that missions can then use that account's resources without the agent ever holding the credential. Each service has one way.

## The two ways

- **The service's sign-in** (Miro). The person is sent to the service, allows the gateway and comes back; the gateway registers its own client at the service the first time someone connects, so nobody declares anything. Tokens obtained this way are short-lived and refreshed by the instance. Google registers no client on the fly: Google Calendar signs in through an OAuth broker the instance is enrolled with, which holds one Google client and keeps no token; a self-hosted instance runs its own ([Your own OAuth broker](/docs/operations/oauth-broker)).
- **With a key pasted by hand** (a token; for Gmail, an app password). The connect page links the token page, says how to create the token, how wide to grant (the widest the service offers for what the account connects: each mission narrows it, so the token is made once), which permissions to tick and who knows the expiry, and the instance checks it with the service before storing anything. A token pasted by hand is a long-lived secret that acts as a person or a service account: its reach is what was granted at the service, and removing the account here deletes nothing there (accepted risk A30 of the [threat model](https://github.com/vaubaan/gateway/blob/main/docs/handbook/threat-model.md#accepted-risks)).

After connecting, the owner labels resources with their environment (`staging`, `prod`) on the account's page, and can give access to a teammate or a team.

**No plugin has yet executed a tool against the real service it models**: every provider call of the test suites is answered by a double ([known limitations](/docs/known-limitations)).
