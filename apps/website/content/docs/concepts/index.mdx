A mission is what a person allows an agent to do through Vaubaan Gateway: which accounts and resources it may touch, which permissions it holds, the limits of each, and which actions ask first. The other words of the interface follow from it.

## Instance

One running Vaubaan Gateway: one process, one embedded database, on your machine or your network, for you and then your team. It signs people in through your identity provider (OpenID Connect), GitHub, Google or Microsoft, or with sign-in accounts it keeps itself, and holds the credentials of the accounts connected to it, encrypted under a master key you keep. There is no hosted service and no tenant.

## Account

Your account at one service — a GitHub organisation through your company's GitHub App, a Supabase organisation, a Stripe account, an SSH host. A person connects it once; its credential stays on the instance. The person who connected it owns it and can give access to a teammate or a team, possibly restricted to some permissions.

## Resource

What a mission selects inside an account: a repository, a project, a database, a DNS zone, a host. An owner can label a resource with an environment such as `staging` or `prod`; some permissions exist only for one environment (`supabase[staging].migration-write`).

## Mission

The unit of delegation. A mission names the accounts and resources the agent may touch, its permissions, the limits of each, and the actions that ask its creator first. It can run once or reload its limits every period, expire, be stopped, and be worked on by teammates, each with their own key.

## Key

The secret an agent presents to the gateway, one per mission and per holder. It opens the execution door of that mission and nothing else: not the interface. It is shown once; the gateway keeps a digest. Replace it or stop the mission at any time.

## Permission

A named risk from a service's catalog, such as `github.merge-protected` or `supabase[prod].row-write`, with the reason it exists and its row: Harmless, Worth a look, Repairable, at a price, or Cannot be repaired, read from five facts (undo, blast radius, data, cost, visible to); the first fact that answers is the reason shown beside it. A mission stores an explicit list of permissions; nothing is ever "all". Every service's permissions are on its [plugin page](/plugins); how the rows and the trust levels decide is explained in [how a permission is decided](/docs/concepts/permissions).

## Macro-action

One thing to do with a service, in its own words — "Read", "Propose", "Ship" — grouping the permissions it needs, each with a base limit. Every permission belongs to one macro-action; "Anything else" holds the uncategorized writes. The person creating a mission ticks macro-actions per account; the mission stores the explicit list of permissions, never the macro-action. Every account also offers "Everything I hold on this account": every permission you may allow there, written out one by one when the mission is created.

## Trust level

What a ticked macro-action is set to: Careful, Trusting or Autonomous. From how hard each permission is to undo, the level decides whether it runs freely, asks first or is left out, and it raises the limits. It only pre-fills: every line stays editable before the mission is created. What each level does with each row: [rows by trust level](/docs/reference/trust-levels).

## Limit

How many times a permission may be used per period and how fast (per minute). A limit that is reached refuses the call with `budget-exhausted` and a link where the mission's creator can give more.

## Ask me first

A permission marked "ask me first" makes each use wait for the mission's creator, who answers under "Waiting for you" in the interface. The gateway never holds a call: it refuses it with `approval-required` and the link of the request; the agent replays the same call once approved. Every refusal and what to do about it: [refusals](/docs/reference/refusals). The other permissions "run freely", within their limits.

## Activity

The record of every call, allowed or refused, with the permissions it needed, whose key made it and the decision taken, and of every change a person made. A call's arguments are kept as a fingerprint only.

## The other words

- **Teammate**: a person added to a mission, with their own key and the mission's shared limits.
- **Vaubaan account**: a person's own account on the gateway, one click, no secret, never shared; a mission on it lets an agent prepare missions in that person's name ([platform management](/docs/missions/platform-management)).
- **Company app**: your company's own app at a service (a GitHub App, a Supabase OAuth app), registered by an administrator once per instance.
- **Administrator**: a person matching the instance's admin rule; configures the instance, never answers another person's requests.

## The same things, in the protocol

The interface, the code and the protocol say account, service and plugin alike; for the other words, tool names, refusal codes, permission ids, the API and the configuration keep the protocol's names: a permission is a risk, a key a mission token, "ask me first" the gate `approval`. The whole table, generated from the specification: [words](/docs/reference/words).
