Seven entries: Now, Waiting for you, Missions, Accounts, Activity, Reference, then Settings. Each page has a small (i) beside its title: it says what the page is for, and links to its part of these docs. Missions and accounts have their own pages: [Missions and agents](/docs/missions), [Accounts](/docs/accounts). The words are those of [Concepts](/docs/concepts).

## Now

The home. What waits for you comes first, answerable where you see it; then what is running, one line per mission; then one line of counts that lead elsewhere. When nothing waits, the page says so in one sentence and what is running is one click away. Before your first mission, it gives the steps to the first account and the first mission instead.

A yes is never given from the home: an approval leads to its request, where the exact call is shown. A refusal runs nothing and may be given at once.

## Waiting for you

What your missions ask of you, oldest first, with one request open beside the list:

- **An approval**: a permission marked "ask me first" was used. The request shows the call — the tool, the resource, the arguments — and who made it. **Approve** lets the agent replay exactly that call; **Refuse** tells it no.
- **A limit reached**: a permission used its limit for the period. Give more, reset it, or refuse.

Only the mission's creator answers; a teammate sees whose answer it waits for. The agent never waits on a held call: it was refused with the request's address, and replays once you have answered ([when a call is refused](/docs/missions#when-a-call-is-refused)). A device can tell you when something waits: [notifications](/docs/missions/notifications).

## Activity

Three tabs: the Overview, the Record and the Timeline.

### The overview

The home of Activity, one block per mission, over today, the last 7 days or the last 30 days (days in UTC): the calls allowed and refused, the requests waiting for an answer, and the bytes sent to the services and received from them. Open a mission's block for the requests per account, the use of each action against its limit in the mission's current period, and who holds its keys with their share of the calls. Above them, what stops working within 7 days: a mission's end, an account whose pasted key ends, an account the service refused — each with where it is fixed. Every number opens the Record or the Timeline filtered on it. You read the missions you created or are a teammate of; an administrator reads every one. Bytes are counted from the version that introduced the Overview: older calls show none.

### The record

The audit record: one line per call, allowed or refused, and per change a person made, newest first. Filter by period, mission, account, tool, decision, person, key holder, agent and kind; open a line for its key, the permissions it touched and what is left, the fingerprint of its arguments and what the agent was told. You read the missions you created, the ones shared with you and the accounts you own; an administrator reads everything. Arguments are never stored, only their fingerprint. How long lines are kept is the instance's [retention](/docs/operations#audit-retention).

### The timeline

What happened in each mission, newest first, in the words of the mission's own page: calls, requests and their answers, resets, limits, gates and keys. Filter by mission, key holder or agent label (or the calls without one); filters apply as soon as you choose them. You read the missions you created or are a teammate of; an administrator reads every one. Events are kept as long as the record.

## Reference

What an agent can do on each service, permission by permission, as the instance's plugins declare it. Read-only: nothing there changes anything. The same pages, with a connect guide for each service, are on this site under [Plugins](/plugins).

## Settings

Your own settings — the theme, **Security**, notifications, signing out everywhere — then, for an administrator, the [Administration](/docs/administration) of the instance.

- **Notifications**: [Notifications on your phone](/docs/missions/notifications).
- **Sign out everywhere** ends every session you have open; your keys and missions are not touched. An agent that prepares missions in your name holds the key of a mission on your Vaubaan account ([platform management](/docs/missions/platform-management)).

## Security

When the instance keeps your account (sign-in accounts), your password; and, whatever your sign-in provider, your second factor. A new second factor gives recovery codes once, on this page: keep them apart.
