---
title: Notifications on your phone
description: Install the interface from the browser, or add a messaging channel or your mail, and be told when a mission asks you something or an account stops serving.
---

The instance can tell you, on your phone's lock screen or your computer, that something waits for you: an approval, a claim for more budget, a permission an agent asks for. It also tells you when an account of yours needs reconnecting or a mission of yours stopped without you. There is no app to download: the interface itself is installed from the browser.

## Turn it on

1. Open **Settings → Notifications** on the device that should receive them.
2. Choose **Receive notifications on this device** and allow notifications when the browser asks.
3. Do the same on each phone or computer you use. Each one is listed, and you can rename or remove it.

On an **iPhone or iPad**, add the page to the Home Screen first (Share, then **Add to Home Screen**), open it from there, then subscribe: Safari sends notifications only to an installed interface.

The home page also offers it the first time something waits for you; **Not now** is remembered by that browser.

## In a messaging channel or by mail

Under **Messaging addresses**, paste the incoming webhook of a channel you read: **Slack**, **Microsoft Teams** (a Workflows webhook), **Discord**, **ntfy**, or **Other** — any https address that accepts a JSON body `{ title, body, url, kind, instance }`. The instance posts a test message first and keeps the address only if the service took it. The address is kept sealed and never shown again: treat it as a password. You can add up to ten, and remove any.

Under **Mail**, turn mail on to receive each notification at your account's address too, when the instance delivers mail.

## What a notification says

- The instance's name, what happened, the mission's or account's name and, for a request, the tool.
- Never the arguments of a call, a secret, a resource or a person's name.
- Opening it leads to the request, the mission or the account. You answer in the interface, signed in, never from the notification.

## What the operator needs

Nothing to configure for devices and messaging addresses. The instance must be served over https, and reach the push services of the browser vendors (Google, Mozilla, Apple, Microsoft) and the messaging services people use on port 443; an address inside a private network is refused. Mail needs the instance's mail relay. An instance that cannot reach them works the same; only the alerts are missing. The home and the inbox always show what waits.
